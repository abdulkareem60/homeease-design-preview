Rationale, tradeoffs and notes

Both directions share the same sample catalogue, prices, descriptions, stock states, cart and WhatsApp behaviour (shared/catalog.js, shared/core.js). They differ in composition, typography, colour, density and a few interaction choices.

A · Warm Everyday

Feel Welcoming, homely, dependable.

  • Composition: editorial split hero with a three-product collage, so merchandise is visible immediately; airy four-column grid; tinted category band; an "in use" desk scene that links to the three products shown.
  • Type: Fraunces (soft serif) for headings gives warmth; Figtree keeps prices, labels and controls plain and readable.
  • Colour: warm cream base, terracotta reserved for primary actions, deep olive footer and top bar.
  • Purchase: pill buttons, colour chips with text labels, add-to-cart opens the cart drawer. No sticky bar; buy controls sit in the flow.
  • Tradeoffs: fewer products per screen; the serif and imagery carry more of the brand, so it depends more on good photography; more scrolling on mobile before the buy button (about 1.3 screens).

B · Clear & Practical

Feel Organised, efficient, confident.

  • Composition: compact intro beside a category index, then a hairline catalogue grid; a mug-vs-tumbler comparison table as the practical section; numbered ordering steps.
  • Type: one grotesk family for clarity; IBM Plex Mono only for specifications, SKUs and counts, which makes facts scannable.
  • Colour: white and light grey, one deep teal accent for actions and focus.
  • Purchase: price and stock on one line, add-to-cart button shows the line total, an "At a glance" spec table is always visible, add-to-cart confirms with a toast and a "View cart" link. A sticky buy bar appears on phones only after the main button scrolls away, and hides at the footer.
  • Tradeoffs: less emotional warmth; can feel like a generic marketplace if photography is inconsistent; denser cards need careful copy (short names and spec lines).

Recommendation

Direction A as the base, borrowing three elements from B. HomeEaseEssentials sells affordable, chosen-for-you home items. A's warmth supports "carefully selected" better than a catalogue look, and it stays credible with a small range. From B, take: the always-visible "At a glance" spec table on the product page, the price-and-stock-on-one-line layout, and the phone-only sticky buy bar. B is the better choice if the catalogue grows past roughly 40–50 products or photography will be mostly plain supplier images.

Sample data and asset provenance

  • All products, prices, stock, SKUs (prefixed SAMPLE-), dimensions and descriptions are invented for review, kept only in shared/catalog.js.
  • Product images are generated in code (shared/images.js) as SVG renders. They are not photographs and are named "(sample render)" in their alternative text. No stock photos were downloaded, and no third-party images are used.
  • Effect on the review: the renders show layout, cropping, colour variants and gallery behaviour accurately, but they look cleaner and more illustrative than real photos. Judge typography, layout and controls with confidence; judge "photography style" only as a direction (A: warm tabletop backdrop; B: neutral studio grey).
  • Fonts: Fraunces, Figtree, Schibsted Grotesk and IBM Plex Mono, all SIL Open Font License, loaded from Google Fonts. Without internet, system fallback fonts are used and the look changes.
  • Icons: a small outline set drawn for this preview.

Known limitations of this preview

  • Only the homepage and one product page (the jewellery tray) are designed. Other products open a quick-view panel instead of a full page.
  • No listing, search results page, checkout, account or admin. Checkout buttons explain that checkout is outside this phase.
  • Search is a live overlay over the 9 sample products, not a results page.
  • The cart is stored in the browser's local storage per direction, so A and B keep separate carts. It may not persist in private windows.
  • Delivery, returns, contact and policy content is marked provisional; business number, hours, email, courier and policies are TBC.
  • Lab checks only: no real-device testing, screen-reader testing or performance measurement has been done yet.
